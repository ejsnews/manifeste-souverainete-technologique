# ENERGY SOVEREIGNTY & INDUSTRIAL RESILIENCE
*Independent strategic briefing — June 2026*

---

## The situation: a dead end no one talks about openly

France imports over €60 billion worth of fossil fuels annually. It heavily subsidizes electrolysis and white hydrogen—two sectors that consume electricity without producing it, strain an already overburdened grid, and offer no carbon sink capabilities. Meanwhile, a French SME, Haffner Energy, has developed and patented a breakthrough technology: decentralized thermolysis of residual biomass. This process simultaneously produces competitive local energy, green hydrogen, sustainable aviation fuel (SAF), and biochar—a solid, measurable carbon sink.

This technology exists. The modules are available. The patents are French. The figures are public.

---

## What this changes in practical terms

Thermolysis of residual biomass (agricultural waste, forestry residues, Class B wood) enables a municipality, hospital, agricultural cooperative, or data center operator to produce its own decarbonized energy—without the need for foundations or building permits, and with an installation time of less than one month.

- Hydrogen production costs range from **€1.2/kg** (using free biomass) to **€2.34/kg** (using 669 kg/h of biomass at **€90/tonne**, without subsidies).
- The produced SAF is already at **price parity with fossil kerosene**, with a trajectory of continuous cost reduction. - The biochar co-product permanently sequesters biogenic carbon in agricultural soils, generating carbon credits with significant market value (**€110–€2,000 per tonne of CO₂**).

Each deployment lowers local energy bills, creates jobs that cannot be offshored, turns resources currently viewed as waste-disposal liabilities into assets—and automatically reduces France's trade deficit.

---

## What the State should do—but isn't doing

Three simple measures could reverse the trend:

**1. An anti-dilution shield**: a sovereign fund targeting strategic listed tech SMEs, banning toxic financing mechanisms (such as OCEANEs or warrants with massive dilutive effects).

**2. A regulatory fast-track**: reducing the administrative review time (ICPE process) to under three months for authorizing these decentralized mobile units at critical infrastructure sites.

**3. Regional momentum**: enabling local authorities, hospitals, and cooperatives to contract directly with these operators, bypassing centralized grid monopolies.

## Silent predation: ignored patents, diluted founders

Filing patents worldwide is not enough to protect a technology when the company holding them is financially on its knees. Haffner Energy spent a decade patenting its thermolysis technology in numerous countries. These patents are public, accessible, and precise. Yet foreign—and potentially domestic—players are using them as a roadmap to replicate the exact same process, simply by swapping out the engineering partner. Without the legal and financial resources to defend themselves in international courts, a patent is nothing more than a piece of paper.

This is not a hypothesis; it is the current reality for an SME whose founders collectively held over 50% of the equity six months ago. Today, they hold **11%**. It was not because they sold, but because the company needed 4 million euros—a paltry sum given the industrial stakes involved—which it could not borrow under normal terms. The only available financing was an ABO mechanism (an arrangement involving warrants linked to bonds—a structure that inherently dilutes the founders' equity), which automatically shifted control of the company away from its creators within a matter of weeks.

This is not an isolated case. It is the standard modus operandi of financial predation targeting listed SMEs with breakthrough technologies: draining cash reserves, blocking access to traditional bank credit, and then offering dilutive financing as the only lifeline. The founders are effectively ousted, the technology remains with the company but without its architects, and the way is paved for an acquisition or an unchallenged copycat move.

France cannot afford to lose—through administrative inertia and the lack of a sovereign financial shield—the few companies that possess patented, world-class disruptive technologies. The cost of a protection fund targeting these ten or twenty strategic firms would be negligible compared to the cost of a decade of additional energy imports or the acquisition of French patents by foreign capital.

## A Narrow Window of Opportunity

The surge in energy demand driven by AI and robotics will create unprecedented market pull over the next five years. If France does not deploy its own low-carbon solutions now, American or Asian capital will step in—using our patents, acquired at "distress-sale" prices.

There is no other virtuous scenario:

- **Nuclear power alone** generates anthropogenic heat without an active carbon sink.
- **Electrolysis** is a net energy consumer on an already saturated grid.
- **Imports** perpetuate dependency and widen the trade deficit.

The French solution exists; it is mature, patented, and profitable without subsidies. All that remains is the decision to deploy it.
